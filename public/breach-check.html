Password Breach Check

Find out whether a password has appeared in a known data breach. Your password is never sent anywhere: your browser sends only the first five characters of a one-way hash to the Have I Been Pwned "Pwned Passwords" service and does the final comparison itself.

How the check protects your privacy

  1. Your browser turns the password into a SHA-1 hash, a fixed-length fingerprint that cannot be reversed.
  2. It sends only the first five characters of that hash to the Pwned Passwords service (run by Have I Been Pwned), together with a request that the reply be padded so its size does not hint at the result.
  3. The service returns the endings of every known breached hash that starts with those five characters, with a count for each.
  4. Your browser looks for the rest of your hash in that list. The match happens on your device.

This design is called k-anonymity. Neither your password nor your full hash leaves your device, and KeyGuard Pro does not receive or store anything you type. The request goes directly from your browser to Have I Been Pwned, which has its own privacy policy.

What the result means

  • Found: the password has appeared in leaked data and attackers test such passwords first. Stop using it everywhere and replace it with a random one from our generator.
  • Not found: it did not appear in the known data. That does not make it strong, because a weak password may simply not have leaked yet. Use a long random password and a password manager.

If a password you use turns up, read what to do after a data breach. As a precaution, avoid testing your most important passwords on any website; test an older or similar one, or change the important one anyway.
