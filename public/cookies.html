Cookie Policy

This page explains the cookies and similar technologies used on keyguard-pro.xyz, what they are for, and how you can control them. It should be read together with the Privacy Policy, which describes the wider picture of what data is and is not collected.

What a cookie is

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store. It can be used to remember a setting, to keep you signed in, or to recognise the same browser on a later visit. Related technologies, such as local storage and pixels, store information in a similar way. Cookies can belong to the site you are visiting, known as first-party cookies, or to another company whose service is embedded in the page, known as third-party cookies.

Cookies set by KeyGuard Pro

We set no cookies of our own. Google Analytics sets first-party measurement cookies (_ga, _ga_<ID>) on our behalf when analytics storage is allowed; Google AdSense sets third-party advertising cookies. The tools themselves set no cookies and use no local storage. Nothing about the passwords you generate or type is kept between page loads, and the tools continue to work if you block all cookies.

Cookies set by Google services

The site loads two Google services, and each may set cookies or read identifiers in your browser:

  • Google AdSense displays the advertising on the site. Google and its partners use cookies to serve ads, to limit how often the same ad is shown, to measure whether ads are seen, and to detect invalid or fraudulent traffic. Where personalised advertising is allowed, cookies may also be used to choose ads based on your earlier visits to this site or to other sites. Google explains this in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.
  • Google Analytics measures how the site is used in aggregate, for example which pages are viewed and roughly which country a visit comes from. It does not receive the text you type into the password fields.

These third-party cookies are controlled by Google, not by KeyGuard Pro. We do not combine them with anything you enter into the tools, because what you enter never leaves your browser.

The breach check and Have I Been Pwned

The optional breach check is the only tool that contacts a third party. When you run a check, your browser computes a SHA-1 hash of the password on your device and sends only the first five characters of that hash to the Pwned Passwords service operated by Have I Been Pwned. The service returns the endings of every known breached hash that begins with those characters, and your browser finishes the comparison locally.

Have I Been Pwned is an independent third party. It receives the five-character prefix and, like any web service, your IP address and standard request information such as your browser's user agent. It never receives your password or the full hash. The lookup needs no cookie from this site: it is a single request that your browser sends directly to Have I Been Pwned, and KeyGuard Pro does not receive or store what you type. Have I Been Pwned's privacy policy explains how it handles requests.

Consent in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

Google's consent message is shown to visitors in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, and asks whether advertising and analytics cookies may be used. Before a choice is made, the site signals a default of "denied" for advertising storage, ad user data, ad personalisation and analytics storage in those regions. Visitors elsewhere receive a default of "granted", which they can change at any time through the same Google controls.

How to control or delete cookies

You can block, delete or limit cookies in your browser settings. The help pages for the main browsers explain where the controls are: Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge all offer a list of stored cookies and an option to block third-party cookies. Clearing cookies removes the ones already set; blocking them prevents new ones. Private or incognito windows generally discard cookies when the window is closed.

To limit personalised advertising specifically, use Google's Ads Settings, or opt out of many third-party vendors' advertising cookies at aboutads.info. To opt out of Google Analytics across sites, Google offers a browser add-on for that purpose. Blocking cookies does not usually remove ads; it generally means the ads you see are less relevant to you.
